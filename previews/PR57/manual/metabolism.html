Metabolism ​

Metabolic heat is the one term of the heat budget that the organism generates. For an ectotherm it is small and follows from body temperature. For an endotherm it is large, and is what the organism adjusts to hold its temperature.

Moving to BiologicalScaling.jl

The metabolic rate equations on this page are allometric, and their home is BiologicalScaling.jl, which already has a wider set (for example standard_metabolic_rate(Squamate(), mass, temperature)). A coming version of this package will take its metabolic rates from there, and the types below will be replaced. The role of metabolism in the heat budget will not change. These equations are descriptive, not physical, and allometric marks that, see Units, dimensions and functional traits.

The parameters ​

MetabolismParameters holds:

FieldMeaningUsed when
modelan equation for metabolic rate from mass and temperaturesolving for temperature
core_temperaturethe core temperature to be heldsolving for metabolic rate
metabolic_heat_flowthe minimum metabolic rate, basal or restingsolving for metabolic rate
q10the factor by which the metabolic rate changes for 10 °C of core temperatureby thermoregulation, when the core temperature is allowed to rise

When temperature is the unknown, solve_temperature calls metabolic_rate(model, mass, core_temperature) at each trial temperature. When metabolic rate is the unknown, no equation is used for it: metabolic_heat_flow is the floor below which a solution means the animal cannot lose its heat, and the rate at which it still breathes, see Temperature or metabolic rate.

The equations ​

Each is a subtype of MetabolicRateEquation, called with metabolic_rate:

EquationForSource
AndrewsPough2squamate reptiles, from mass and body temperature, standard or restingAndrews and Pough (1985), their equation 2
Kleiberbasal rate of mammals, 3.39M0.75 W with M in kgKleiber (1947)
McKechnieWolfbasal rate of birdsMcKechnie and Wolf (2004)
PlantDarkRespirationdark respiration of leaves, from mass and temperatureReich et al. (2006)
nothingno metabolic heat
julia
using HeatExchange, Unitful

metabolic_rate(Kleiber(), 65.0u"kg"), metabolic_rate(McKechnieWolf(), 30.0u"g"),
metabolic_rate(AndrewsPough2(), 40.0u"g", u"K"(30.0u"°C"))
(77.61841630904179 W, 0.33666201574394194 W, 0.01916394439316384 W)

The rate of an ectotherm is far below that of an endotherm of the same mass, and rises steeply with temperature:

julia
temperatures = 5.0:1.0:45.0   # °C
fig, ax = figure_axis("Body temperature (°C)", "Metabolic rate (mW)")
for (state, label) in ((0.0, "standard"), (1.0, "resting"))
    model = AndrewsPough2(; metabolic_state = state)
    lines!(ax, temperatures, [ustrip(u"mW", metabolic_rate(model, 40.0u"g", u"K"(T * u"°C"))) for T in temperatures];
           linewidth = 2, label)
end
axislegend(ax; position = :lt)
fig

The equation of Andrews and Pough is

V˙O2=M1mM210M3Tb10M4

in ml of oxygen per hour, with mass m in g and body temperature Tb in °C, held between 1 and 50 °C. The constants are the fields mass_normalisation, mass_exponent, thermal_sensitivity and metabolic_state of AndrewsPough2, with metabolic_state 0 for the standard rate and 1 for the resting rate. To add an equation, define a subtype of MetabolicRateEquation and a method of metabolic_rate.

Oxygen and heat ​

Metabolic rate is measured as oxygen consumed and needed here as heat produced. O2_to_Joules and Joules_to_O2 convert between them with an OxygenJoulesConversion:

ConversionEnergy per volume of oxygen
Typical20.1 J/ml
Kleiber19615.0, 4.5 or 4.7 kcal/l for a respiratory quotient of 1 or more, between 0.7 and 1, or 0.7 or less (Kleiber 1961)
julia
oxygen = Joules_to_O2(Typical(), 100.0u"W", 0.8)
uconvert(u"ml/hr", oxygen), uconvert(u"ml/hr", Joules_to_O2(Kleiber1961(), 100.0u"W", 0.8))
(17910.447761194027 mL hr^-1, 19120.458891013386 mL hr^-1)

The oxygen consumed sets the air breathed, and so the heat and water lost in respiration, see Evaporation and respiration. This ratio is one of the three places where the budgets of heat and mass are coupled, see Flows of mass.

Metabolism and temperature ​

Thermal performance curves that fall away at high temperature give the relation of metabolic rate to temperature in more detail, from ThermalPhysiology.jl.

The metabolic rate of an animal over its life, as it grows and reproduces, is the subject of Dynamic Energy Budget theory, which the ectotherm model of NicheMapR includes (Kearney and Porter 2020). It is outside this package, and is to enter the ecosystem through AnimalMapper.jl, by way of DEBtool_J.jl, where it will couple the flows of mass and give heat production from first principles, see Flows of mass.
