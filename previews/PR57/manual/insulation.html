Insulation ​

Fur and feathers are porous: a mass of fibres with still air between them. Much of the challenge of modelling the heat budget of an endotherm lies in the movement of heat through this layer, which is by conduction along the fibres and through the air, and by radiation between the fibres, all at once (Porter et al. 1994, Kearney et al. 2021). This page describes how the coat is specified and how its conductivity is found. The word fur is used throughout, and applies to feathers, hair and clothing as well.

Specifying a coat ​

A coat is described in two places, which must agree.

The body has a FibrousLayer from BiophysicalGeometry.jl, with a depth, a fibre diameter and a fibre density. From these come the outer radius and area of the animal, and the area of skin left bare between the fibres.

The traits have an InsulationParameters, which holds a FibreProperties for the dorsal and for the ventral surface:

conductivitythermal conductivity of the fibre material, 0.209 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ for keratinKHAIR

and two values for the whole coat:

julia
using HeatExchange, BiophysicalGeometry, Unitful

fibres = FibreProperties(; diameter = 30.0u"μm", length = 23.9u"mm", density = 3000.0u"cm^-2", depth = 9.0u"mm",
                           reflectance = 0.3, conductivity = 0.209u"W/m/K")
insulation_pars = InsulationParameters(; dorsal = fibres, ventral = fibres, depth_compressed = 9.0u"mm",
                                         longwave_depth_fraction = 1.0)

The body takes the depth, diameter and density from the same values. Where the dorsal and ventral coats differ, the body is given their mean, weighted by the fraction of the surface that is ventral, as NicheMapR does:

julia
fur = FibrousLayer(fibres.depth, fibres.diameter, fibres.density)
body = Body(Ellipsoid(10.0u"kg", 1000.0u"kg/m^3", 2.7, 2.7), CompositeInsulation(fur, FatLayer(0.0, 901.0u"kg/m^3")))

The coat drawn to scale, with the fraction of the skin that the bases of its fibres cover:

julia
plot_insulation_properties(fur)

Conduction through the coat ​

insulation_properties computes the properties of the coat that the heat budget needs, by the theory of Conley and Porter (1986). It corresponds to the subroutines IRPROP and GETKFUR of NicheMapR.

julia
properties = insulation_properties(insulation_pars, u"K"(20.0u"°C"), 0.3)   # temperature in the coat, ventral fraction
properties.conductivities.dorsal
0.028656189990380776 W K^-1 m^-1

This effective conductivity is that of the fibres and the air together. The fibres are treated as a regular array of cylinders. Heat is conducted in parallel through fibre and air along the direction of the fibres, and in series across them, and the effective conductivity is the mean of the two, held between the conductivity of air and that of the fibre. The air between the fibres is taken to be still, and its conductivity rises with its temperature, so the result depends on the temperatures in the coat. The fibres are much the better conductor, so anything that puts more fibre into the layer raises the conductivity:

julia
air_conductivity = 0.0257u"W/m/K"   # at 20 °C
vary(; kw...) = ustrip(u"W/m/K", insulation_thermal_conductivity(FibreProperties(; diameter = fibres.diameter,
    length = fibres.length, density = fibres.density, depth = fibres.depth, reflectance = 0.3,
    conductivity = fibres.conductivity, kw...), air_conductivity).effective_conductivity)

fig = Figure(size = (760, 300))
diameters = 2.0:2.0:100.0

fig

The dashed line is still air. The ranges stop short of the point at which the fibres touch. A deeper coat of the same fibres has a lower conductivity, because the fibres, of fixed length, stand more upright and less of each lies within a given depth.

insulation_properties returns an InsulationProperties, with values for the dorsal surface, the ventral surface and their average, each as a BodyRegionValues:

FieldContent

conductivitieseffective conductivities
absorption_coefficients, optical_thicknesshow far longwave radiation penetrates the coat
conductivity_compressedeffective conductivity of the coat pressed to depth_compressed
insulation_testzero if there is no coat, which sends the solvers down the bare-skin path

Radiation within the coat ​

Each fibre also radiates to its neighbours, and where there is a gradient of temperature this carries heat down it. The effect is that of an added conductivity, which rises with the cube of the temperature and falls as the fibres become denser and absorb the radiation over a shorter distance (Conley and Porter 1986):

krad=16σT33β,β=0.67πρeffd

where β is the absorption coefficient of the coat, ρeff the density of fibres allowing for their angle and d their diameter. The conductivity of the fur is the sum

kfur=keff+krad

It depends on the skin and fur surface temperatures, which are being solved for, and is recomputed at each step of the solution. The value at the solution is in the output as insulation_conductivity, beside the effective conductivity alone. Measured conductivities of fur include both parts.

The surface of the coat ​

Heat conducted through a coat on a cylinder of length L from the skin at radius Rs to the outer surface at Rfa is

Qfur=2πkfurL(Ts−Tfa)ln⁡(Rfa/Rs)

with the corresponding forms for a sphere and an ellipsoid. At the outer surface this heat, with the sunlight absorbed there, leaves by convection, longwave radiation and the evaporation of any water on the fur. These are the equations that solve_part_heat_balance holds, and the skin and surface temperatures are found together, see Solving a heat balance. The method is that of Mathewson and Porter (2013), with the surface balance solved to convergence by a Newton iteration.

radiant_temperature gives the temperature at which the coat exchanges longwave radiation. With a longwave_depth_fraction of 1 it is the temperature of the outer surface. A smaller fraction places the exchange within the coat. That option is inherited from NicheMapR, and is not yet reliable in this version: the closed form is singular as the depth approaches the surface, see Layers as a radial graph.

Lying on the ground ​

Where an animal lies on the ground its coat is pressed flat. The fraction of the surface in contact, conduction_fraction, then loses heat by a second path: from the skin through the compressed coat, of depth depth_compressed and conductivity conductivity_compressed, to the substrate. The two paths, through the free coat to the air and through the compressed coat to the ground, are in parallel, and compressed_radiant_temperature and insulation_radiant_temperature hold the solutions for each shape. It is not a small correction: for a 1 kg animal under a cold sky on warm ground, a contact of 30 % of the surface carried about a quarter of the heat exchanged, see Layers as a radial graph.

Back and belly ​

The dorsal and ventral coats can differ in every property. The solvers treat the animal as two sides, and solve the surface of each with its own coat and its own surroundings: the dorsal side sees the sky and any vegetation overhead and takes the direct sunlight, and the ventral side sees the ground and takes the sunlight reflected from it. ventral_fraction in RadiationParameters is the fraction of the surface that is ventral, used for the average properties of the coat. The output has the temperatures, the conductivity and every heat flow for each side:

julia
thin = FibreProperties(; diameter = 30.0u"μm", length = 23.9u"mm", density = 3000.0u"cm^-2", depth = 3.0u"mm",
                         reflectance = 0.3, conductivity = 0.209u"W/m/K")
traits = example_heat_exchange_traits(;
    shape_pars = body.shape,
    insulation_pars = InsulationParameters(; dorsal = fibres, ventral = thin, depth_compressed = 3.0u"mm",
                                             longwave_depth_fraction = 1.0),
    metabolism_pars = example_metabolism_pars(; metabolic_heat_flow = metabolic_rate(Kleiber(), 10.0u"kg")),
)
mean_fur = FibrousLayer(6.0u"mm", 30.0u"μm", 3000.0u"cm^-2")
animal = Organism(Body(body.shape, CompositeInsulation(mean_fur, FatLayer(0.0, 901.0u"kg/m^3"))), traits)
environment = (; environment_pars = example_environment_pars(),
                 environment_vars = example_environment_vars(; air_temperature = u"K"(5.0u"°C")))
out = solve_metabolic_rate(animal, environment, u"K"(34.0u"°C"), u"K"(5.0u"°C"))

The heat conducted to the skin is given for each side as if the whole animal were that side, and the two are averaged by the view that each side has. For a body in which the back and the belly are separate parts, see Bodies of many parts.

Fat ​

Fat under the skin is a second layer of insulation, inside the skin where fur is outside it. It is given to the body as a FatLayer, a fraction of the body mass at a density, and to the traits as fat_fraction, fat_density and fat_conductivity in InternalConductionParameters. See Layers as a radial graph.
