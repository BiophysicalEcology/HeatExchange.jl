Get started ​

HeatExchange.jl solves the heat budget of an organism in a given environment, with every quantity a Unitful.jl quantity.

julia
using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/BiophysicalEcology/HeatExchange.jl")

An organism ​

An Organism is a body and a set of traits. The body is a shape from BiophysicalGeometry.jl, sized from a mass and a density. The traits are the surface properties and physiology that the heat budget needs. Here is a 40 g lizard with bare skin and the default traits of an ectotherm:

julia
using HeatExchange, BiophysicalGeometry, Unitful

shape = DesertIguana(40.0u"g", 1000.0u"kg/m^3")
body = Body(shape, Naked())
lizard = Organism(body, example_ectotherm_heat_exchange_traits(; shape_pars = shape))

An environment ​

An environment has two parts: environment_vars, the conditions that change from hour to hour, and environment_pars, the properties of the site. Here the lizard is in the sun on a cool morning with a light wind:

julia
environment_vars = example_environment_vars(;
    air_temperature = u"K"(20.0u"°C"),
    wind_speed = 1.0u"m/s",
    global_radiation = 400.0u"W/m^2",
    zenith_angle = 30.0u"°",
)
environment_pars = example_environment_pars()
environment = (; environment_pars, environment_vars)

example_environment_vars sets the sky, ground and substrate to the air temperature unless told otherwise. See Parameters for every field, and Environments and the ecosystem for where real values come from.

Solve for body temperature ​

A lizard makes little metabolic heat and does not adjust it to defend a core temperature, so its body temperature is whatever balances the heat it gains and loses. solve_temperature finds it:

julia
out = solve_temperature(lizard, environment)
u"°C"(out.core_temperature) # default output is in Kelvin
22.91583497727379 °C

The lizard is above air temperature. The terms of its heat budget show why:

TermValue
solar_flow1.528 W
longwave_flow_in4.006 W
metabolic_heat_flow0.01031 W
respiration_heat_flow0.0006944 W
evaporation_heat_flow0.01867 W
longwave_flow_out4.166 W
convection_heat_flow0.9314 W
conduction_flow0.4273 W
heat_balance4.33e-7 W
![](jebomry.png){width=640px height=360px}

Sunlight and longwave radiation from the sky and ground come in, and leave as longwave radiation, by convection to the air, and by conduction to the ground, which is at air temperature here and touches a tenth of the lizard. Metabolism and evaporation are a small part of this animal's budget. The water it loses is in out.mass_balance. See Solving a heat balance.

Solve for metabolic rate ​

A mammal or bird holds its core temperature steady, and changes the heat it produces. Here is a 65 kg animal with 2 mm of fur, the default of example_heat_exchange_traits, in still air at 0 °C:

julia
shape = Ellipsoid(65.0u"kg", 1000.0u"kg/m^3", 1.1, 1.1)
fibres = FibreProperties(; diameter = 30.0u"μm", length = 23.9u"mm", density = 3000.0u"cm^-2", depth = 2.0u"mm",
                           reflectance = 0.2, conductivity = 0.209u"W/m/K")
fur = FibrousLayer(fibres.depth, fibres.diameter, fibres.density)
fat = FatLayer(0.0, 901.0u"kg/m^3")
body = Body(shape, CompositeInsulation(fur, fat))

traits = example_heat_exchange_traits(;
    shape_pars = shape,
    insulation_pars = InsulationParameters(; dorsal = fibres, ventral = fibres, depth_compressed = fibres.depth,
                                             longwave_depth_fraction = 1.0),
)
mammal = Organism(body, traits)

cold = (; environment_pars = example_environment_pars(),
          environment_vars = example_environment_vars(; air_temperature = u"K"(0.0u"°C")))

The fur appears twice. The body needs its depth, for the outer area and radius. The traits need the properties of its fibres too, for how well it conducts heat, see Insulation.

solve_metabolic_rate finds the metabolic rate that holds the core at the temperature in metabolism_pars, 37 °C here. It takes first guesses of the skin and fur surface temperatures:

julia
result = solve_metabolic_rate(mammal, cold, u"K"(34.0u"°C"), u"K"(0.0u"°C"))
result.energy_flows.metabolic_heat_flow
112.27827373584209 W

The basal metabolic rate of an animal of this mass is 77.6 W (Kleiber 1947), so it must produce about 1.4 times basal to stay warm. The skin and the outer surface of the fur are found at the same time:

julia
u"°C"(result.thermoregulation.skin_temperature), u"°C"(result.thermoregulation.insulation_temperature)
(20.0339850137928 °C, 13.196993336535343 °C)

The result has four groups of output, thermoregulation, morphology, energy_flows and mass_flows, see Temperature or metabolic rate.

Any combination ​

The two solvers are not tied to kinds of animal. The furred body can be solved for its temperature, that of an animal that has stopped thermoregulating, with its metabolic rate at basal:

julia
torpid = solve_temperature(mammal, cold)
u"°C"(torpid.thermoregulation.core_temperature)
27.37230460862287 °C
